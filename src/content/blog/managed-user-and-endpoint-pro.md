Most cyber attacks on small and regional organisations start in one of two places. Either it's a person, through their email and Microsoft 365 account. Or it's a device that's missing an update or running something it shouldn't.

Our two Managed IT Complete 2026 services cover each of those. SupportNeT Managed User looks after your people. SupportNeT Managed Endpoint Pro looks after your devices. Most staff have one of each: a Managed User for the person, and a Managed Endpoint Pro for every computer they use.

None of the tools are exotic, and we already run them every day. What you're paying for is our team setting them up properly, watching them and acting on what they find.

## Managed User: protection for each person

Billed per user, per month. It covers:

- **Email security.** Every incoming email is checked for phishing, spam and impersonation. Anything suspicious gets a coloured warning banner, so people can see it needs a second look.
- **Awareness training.** Short, regular modules on spotting scams, plus safe practice phishing emails. Anyone who clicks gets a follow-up lesson, and you get reports on where training is needed most.
- **Dark web monitoring.** We watch for your staff's email addresses turning up in breach data, so a stolen password can be changed before it's used.
- **Microsoft 365 backup, kept indefinitely.** Email, OneDrive, SharePoint and Teams data is backed up separately from Microsoft, so we can restore something deleted by mistake or lost to ransomware.
- **Microsoft 365 activity monitoring and 24/7 threat detection and response.** Unusual sign-ins, hidden email forwarding rules and account takeovers are watched around the clock. When a serious threat is confirmed, it can sign the attacker out, lock the account or pull a phishing email from every inbox, under rules we agree with you beforehand.
- **Unlimited business-hours support.** Call or log a ticket as often as you need, with no extra charge per request.

Managed User needs Microsoft 365 Business Premium.

## Managed Endpoint Pro: protection for each device

Billed per device, per month. It covers:

- **Device management and remote access.** We watch each device's health and can fix most problems remotely, usually without interrupting the person using it.
- **Antivirus and endpoint detection and response.** Antivirus blocks known malware. EDR watches what programs actually do, so it can spot ransomware behaviour and isolate an infected device from the network.
- **Endpoint backup.** If a laptop is lost, stolen or fails, the person's files can be restored.
- **Patching.** Windows and common business software are updated on a managed schedule, with restarts handled so they don't disrupt work.
- **Vulnerability management.** Regular checks for missing patches, weak settings and unsupported software, ranked by risk.
- **Application allowlisting and admin control.** Only software you've approved can run, and everyday admin rights are removed from staff. If malware arrives by email or USB stick, it can't start.

## Why the two overlap

No single tool stops every attack, so the two services overlap on purpose. Take ransomware. Application allowlisting stops unapproved programs from running. EDR detects ransomware behaviour and isolates the device. Backups mean we can restore clean files if the worst happens.

## Where this sits against the Essential Eight

The Australian Cyber Security Centre's Essential Eight is the baseline most Australian organisations get measured against. Between them, the two services give you tools and management for five of the eight: application control, patching applications, patching operating systems, restricting administrative privileges, and regular backups. Multi-factor authentication, Office macro settings and user application hardening are set up in Microsoft 365 and Windows, and we can review those with you when you start.
